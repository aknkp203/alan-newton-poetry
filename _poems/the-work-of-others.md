# The Work of Others

As I age, the universe scares me.
Take black holes,
millions of bloated star-carcasses
drifting like unburied soldiers 
across galactic battlefields,
gripping light and force in their fists –
zombies lurking in the zodiac.

Even visible bodies haunt me.
If I stare at the sun too long,
my eyes will crackle, coagulate,
become two rubbery fried eggs. 
Andromeda is an oil smear on my Ray-Bans
I can’t rub away.
One day I may mistake the Ursas 
for two friendly clumps of light.
What bright eyes you have! 
I’ll say, reaching up to hug them,
ignorant of the claws
hidden beneath heaven’s fur.

Today trips to the doctor bewilder me
for reasons beyond searching for a spot 
in the hospital parking deck.
Every prod, poke, or cut meant
to make me whole leaves me 
feeling less.
Every pill or potion I swallow –
the chemicals, coatings, kraft-paper packaging
in my box of colonoscopy prep –
comes from the work of others
I neither know nor wholly trust.

My work is coaxing thoughts into images,
images into lines,
lines into stanzas,
to build worlds my friends and wife 
can enter without fear.
There I can shape 
the darkness waiting for me,
give my grave the boundaries
of words.
